# Brand name research, round 3 (pattern-led)
Date: 19 Oct 2026. Earlier rounds: BRAND_NAMES.md, BRAND_NAMES_2.md (none of those names are reused).

## Read this first (honest caveats)
- **This is NOT a legal trademark search.** IP India, WIPO, USPTO and EUIPO were not searched. Have a trademark attorney check Classes 9, 12, 25, 35, 42 and sound-alike marks before buying a domain or filing.
- **Domains:** .com via Verisign RDAP, .in via NIXI RDAP, 19 Oct 2026. "Free" = registry returned 404 (no registration). It is not a guarantee: names can be premium/reserved or taken any minute. .co and social handles were NOT checked. Nothing was bought. (`whois`/`dig` are not installed on the box, so RDAP was used.)
- **Web checks** were one exact-name WebSearch per name. Absence of hits is weak evidence. App Store / Play Store and Instagram/X/YouTube were not searched directly.
- **Languages:** my own reading of Hindi, Punjabi, English, Spanish, Arabic/Urdu, Japanese, Lithuanian etc. Get native Punjabi and Hindi speakers to say each name aloud.
- **Search effort:** about 9,000 strings were tested on .com. Real words and smooth names are almost all taken (Round 1 and 2 found the same). Survivors are coined or respelled words built on real roots.

### Academic evidence on name type and length
- Real-word brand names are common and descriptive/suggestive names tend to be liked and recalled easily, but invented names can be recalled better because they are distinctive (Hunts, Heinz and Fries, 2015, Applied Cognitive Psychology; Lowrey et al., 2002, Psychology and Marketing; Zhang/UCLA chapter on real vs coined names).
- Brand name length: a 2025-26 VUB policy brief (Zee, Willems, Brengman, Desmet) says the "two syllable rule" is oversimplified. Longer invented names are recognised better; for liking, about 3-4 phonemes is best.
- Practitioner view: Ola's founder wanted a name that "should not mean anything" so it can stretch to new categories (Economic Times).

### Patterns that correlate with memorable global brands in this field
1. **A short, real root with a story, lightly altered.** Ather (aether), Rever (rêver), Rivian (Indian River), Kalk (limestone), Ola (hello). The alteration (drop a letter, respell, add a suffix) makes it ownable while keeping a meaning you can tell in one sentence.
2. **Two or three syllables is the sweet spot, 5-7 letters.** Ather, Ola, Rever, Zero, Rivian, Forcite, Strava, Cardo, Revolt. Only legacy names are long (Royal Enfield, Ultraviolette).
3. **Soft, open sound, easy first-time spelling.** Most successful names are pronounceable from the spelling in any language: vowels in every syllable, few awkward clusters. Hard Z/V/X clusters (Round 2 style) are rare in the winners.
4. **A meaning that is positive and not product-literal** (energy, dream, hello, strive, foresight). Descriptive names like Calimoto work for an app but box in a hardware company.
5. **Name works as a platform, not just an app.** Ather (energy), Rivian, Zero, Ola all stretched past their first product. A name with "moto/ride" in it would limit the roadmap (app, then devices, then bike).
6. **Origin story is part of the brand** (Rivian, Kalk, Royal Enfield). That fits a Punjab founder using a Punjabi/Sanskrit/Urdu root and respelling it for global readers.
7. **Domain availability shaped names** (Ola bought its domain for Rs 1,000; Ather dropped the "e"). Expect to coin/respell.

- **Pavanix** (.in taken), **Meghix** (.com lookup failed, not retested).
- **Dhrovo**: Dhrovo Oil & Lubricant Pvt Ltd (Indian engine-oil additives, 2021): too close to vehicles.
- **Sagarex**: SAGAREX is an India-Sri Lanka Coast Guard exercise name.
- **Chakravo**: also a Kutch village and a Chakravo.com site record; **Shikhro**: Gujarati word (peak) used in songs/titles.
- **Vyomia** (Vyomia Qualia, Indian site), **Vyomaro** (Vyomora Tech, Vyomara.com): active near-matches.
- **Nabhion**: "nabhi" = navel in Hindi, bad sound-alike.
- **Pragaro**: Lithuanian "pragaras" = hell, plus a Lithuanian company. **Rakshon**: sounds like Hindi "rakshason" (demons). **Parwazar**: sounds like Parazar app. **Udaano**: too close to Udaan (Indian B2B unicorn).
- Dozens of attractive real-word-derived options (Aelo, Ekaro, Avero, Dagaro, Dhunik, Pathik, Parwaz, Udaan, Safaro, Gativ etc.) have .com registered.

## Ranking: top 6
1. **Parwazo**: best fit with the patterns (real root + simple suffix, 3 syllables, open vowels). Meaning "flight" suits app, devices and a future e-bike, and it carries a Punjabi/Urdu story that is not product-literal. Cleanest search results.
2. **Yodhra**: short, strong, strongly Indian but easy to say; warrior fits riders/helmets. Only conflict is unrelated animation firms.
3. **Kazeara**: most "global" in feel (Japanese wind root, Strava-style respell), no negative meaning anywhere checked; weaker India story.
4. **Kadamra**: friendly, easy, "step/stride" fits navigation and community; clean results.
5. **Ashvano**: horse/horsepower story works for a bike brand, 3 syllables; only a gamer-handle conflict.
6. **Vyomon**: sky/space gives wide room for devices; keep an eye on VyomOne AI and the crowded "Vyom-" prefix.
Honourable: Kavacho for the helmet line only if KAV is cleared; Turangia as a longer, more recognisable option.
